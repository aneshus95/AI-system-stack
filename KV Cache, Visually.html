3. Why old K and V never change

A causal transformer masks the future: cat can't attend to sat, on or the. Adding tokens later can't change cat's hidden state, so at every layer:

Kcat = Xcat·WK   Vcat = Xcat·WV   (same every step)

Without a cache, step 5 recomputes K₁:₅ and V₁:₅ (and the whole earlier transformer computation). With a cache it computes only K₅, V₅ and appends them.

K,V projections over N steps: N(N+1)/2 without a cache, N with one. The gap grows quadratically.

4. Why we don't cache Q

The new token needs a fresh question: Q₅ against all keys, then a weighted sum of all values:

O5 = softmax( Q5·K1:5T / √dk ) · V1:5

Q₁…Q₄ already produced their outputs (the brown rows in the picture). Nothing later reads them, so caching them wastes memory. K and V are different: every future query reads them.

Library analogy. You bring a new question each time (Q). The index (K) and the books (V) stay on the shelf so you don't rebuild them.

5. The catch: attention still looks at everything

The cache removes recomputation of the past, not attention to the past. Each new token still does work proportional to n per layer, and the cache must live in memory.

Defaults give about 2 GiB for one 4,096-token sequence. Serve many users and the cache dominates GPU memory. Fewer KV heads (grouped-query attention) shrinks it directly.

Takeaways

  • New token: compute Q, K, V once; append K, V to the cache.
  • Cache K, V: every future query needs the past tokens' keys (to score) and values (to mix).
  • Skip Q: old queries already did their job.
  • Saves recomputation, not attention work. Costs memory that grows with length, layers and heads.
Interview check

Why does a token's K stay fixed? Causal masking: its hidden state depends only on earlier tokens.

Why is decoding memory-bound? Each step reads the whole cache for little arithmetic.

How to shrink the cache? Grouped-query or multi-query attention, quantized KV, sliding windows, paged allocation.
